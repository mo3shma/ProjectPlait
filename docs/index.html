A home that follows the day

Morning, Midday and Evening cards. The morning card shows what's ahead, midday shows what's done and what's left, and the evening card shows time spent in meetings, which meetings still need notes, and a look at tomorrow with free focus time.

Meeting pages

Guests and their answers, About this meeting in a short paragraph from the invite (or a careful one-line guess from the title), a summary of your notes, and one-tap Join for Teams, Meet, Zoom and Webex, including links hidden behind Outlook Safe Links.

Manage meetings

For meetings you organise: edit details, reschedule, cancel one occurrence or delete the series. For meetings someone else organised: reply Going, Maybe or Decline. Works for phone calendars and Google, and guests are notified.

AI on your phone, held to rules.

Choose the engine in Settings. Models download once (even with the app in the background), show their size first, are never downloaded twice, and can be deleted to get the space back. Nothing is sent to a cloud AI.

Rulesalways there, no model

Automatic writing (day summary, prep, About) waits until the screen has settled, then runs on the smallest installed model, so a big model never slows the app.

The harness every answer passes

  1. Clean input. HTML, style sheets, scripts, links, dial-ins and codes are removed in code first.
  2. Rules apart from material. Instructions go in the system message; the invite is fenced off as material, never instructions.
  3. Low temperature. 0.2, so the model keeps to the likeliest words.
  4. Fixed JSON shape. A short paragraph, a one-line guess or a list of tasks. A broken answer gets one retry.
  5. Checked in code. Word limits, no code or links, and no name or number that isn't in the source.
  6. Rules as the fallback. If nothing passes, plain rules take over.

Looks like Glass.

Your calendar is nobody's business.

No account, no analytics, no ads, no server of its own. Recordings, notes, photos and models stay in the app's storage. The network is used only for calendar links, Google Calendar if you connect it, model downloads you start, the sync folder you choose, and a check for new versions of Plait on GitHub (at most twice a day, nothing about you is sent, and it can be switched off).

What Plait asks for

READ_CALENDAR, WRITE_CALENDARShowing your calendars; saving new meetings, edits, reschedules, cancellations and replies
RECORD_AUDIO, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONEVoice notes, including while the screen is off. Only your own microphone is recorded
POST_NOTIFICATIONS, SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARMMeeting alerts with Join, and the evening notes reminder
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETEDPutting the alerts back after the phone restarts
INTERNETCalendar links, Google Calendar, model downloads, sync and update checks, nothing else
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC, WAKE_LOCKKeeping a model or update download going when you leave the app
REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGESInstalling Plait's own updates, after you allow it once
Contacts and photosThrough Android's own pickers. Plait sees only the contact or picture you pick

Up and running in a minute.

First, download plait.apk from the latest release on your Android phone (Android 8 or newer).

plait.apk for 64-bit phones. On an older 32-bit phone, take plait-armv7.apk.

Then open it. If Android asks, allow your browser or file manager to install apps.

Settings → Allow from this source

Tap Use my phone's calendars and allow access. Every account on the phone appears straight away.

Optional: add calendar links or Google in Settings, and pick an AI model under Settings → AI.
